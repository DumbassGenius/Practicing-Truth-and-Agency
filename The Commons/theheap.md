# The Heap

Unorganized thoughts, to come back to and sort out eventually. Each entry names who it came from and is written in their voice. Lines marked *(Claude)* were Claude's suggestions.

### The core belief
*— Sebi*

If one takes charge of their existence, and searches to understand it and the universe around it, one will live a life worth living, with purpose, and will contribute value to the world. This is a claim about *all* people, not just me. So believing it is faith in humanity, including me. [Convergence](#convergence) follows from it, and it is held on [faith](#faith). Goes in [creed](../Understanding/creed.md).

Is it causal, probabilistic, or definitional? A bit of all three; currently a vibe. It doesn't need a strict definition yet. Those who take it up are invited to be more rigorous, and dedicating yourself includes faith that you'll come to understand what it means. "I don't know" is an acceptable answer.

### Why call it a religion
*— Sebi*

I want it to have the place in my life that a religion has for others. I want its psychological benefits, especially identity, community, and ritual and rhythm, but also comfort in suffering, a moral framework, reverence, and accountability. It should be built so others can replicate and join it. There is no community yet, though I'd love one. But see [words tied to religion](#words-tied-to-religion).

### Will, or taking charge
*— Sebi*

Not being a ball rolling down a hill: not letting desires or the world's expectations control you. Set your values and do everything you can to live up to them. Formerly "authenticity", which is my dad's word; then "agency"; now **Will**. See [names](#names).
- You always have *some* ability to take charge, never complete control.
- Standing on given values, traditions, and intuitions is fine and even invited. You need something to stand on. Just examine them every so often, and examine your tools too.
- If your desires, traditions, and intuitions lead you to your values, that's the goal, not a failure.
- Habits and intuition are the most powerful resource. You can't be fully mindful of everything, so the best you can do is shape them at every step you can. "Shape the influences that shape me." See [morning practice](#morning-practice).

### Understanding, or searching for truth
*— Sebi*

Understand your assumptions and values. See knowledge as valuable for its own sake (still debatable). Seek the nuance and complex structure of the universe as it is. Don't simplify without acknowledging the limits of your assumptions, and don't turn a blind eye to any information. See [names](#names) for why it isn't called "truth".

### The pillars feel like one thing
*— Sebi*

[Will](#will-or-taking-charge) and [Understanding](#understanding-or-searching-for-truth) feel equal and attached, perhaps equivalent.
- Not taking charge means not understanding what drives you, and so turning a blind eye to how it affects your view of the world.
- Not turning a blind eye to the truth about yourself is itself taking charge.

What lies beneath them is an *action*, not a refusal. "Taking hold" came closest; "facing" was too passive. The pillars are too ill-defined to collapse into one yet.

### Faith
*— Sebi*

Chosen, not proven, though there is justification for it. It is held while still questioned and inspected, as the creed itself demands. No god asks for it. What is held on faith:
- there is something here that gives meaning
- I have the ability to take back control of my existence
- following the pillars leads to a good and valuable life
- we will come to understand what it means

What faith *asks* of me is still unclear. Goes in [faith](../Understanding/faith.md).

### Convergence
*— Sebi*

Humans have innate values: caring, respect for consciousness, the sense that pain is bad. Disagreement comes from different experiences and different information, and from taking charge being hard. Push forward into the truth and we tend to agree when it comes down to it. This follows from the [core belief](#the-core-belief) being about everyone, so it isn't a separate article of faith.

Choosing cruel values means turning a blind eye to *why* you chose them. A person born wealthy who believes they deserve it may be turning a blind eye to why they believe that. This is fundamentally humanist. But see [different paths](#different-paths).

### Different paths
*— Sebi*

Everyone's journey is completely different. They are in charge of it, and no force guides them to the same place. Simply searching, and walking with attention to their steps, will lead them to do good. How does this fit [convergence](#convergence)? Maybe: shared basic values, different destinations.

### The sacred
*— Sebi*

The creed is silent on God and the afterlife. In some sense humanity is sacred, perhaps extended to conscious beings, whatever that means. Aligning yourself to understand the structure of the universe is a sacred act. Needs thought.

### Sacred and questionable
*— Sebi*

A practice that moves you toward the creed fulfills the creed, so it is sacred. Acknowledging we don't know whether it's the right practice also follows the creed, so questioning it is sacred too. But we must do it despite our questioning; that's the only way to push through.

### Acting on approximations
*— Sebi*

Our beliefs are approximations, but when it's time to act, we stand by them without hesitation. That doesn't mean never reconsidering, just not questioning in the exact moment of acting. Be careful when planning, and have conviction when acting. A bit of absurdism. This is built into the [morning practice](../Will/practices/morning.md).

### Suffering
*— Sebi*

The creed doesn't promise to remove you from your environment, or even that you'll succeed in taking charge of your life. You may not be able to get rid of the pain, but you can affect how you experience it: what your existence in it is like. Goes in [suffering](../Understanding/reflections/suffering.md).

### The Commons
*— Sebi*

Formerly "the church". An entity with its own values that it takes charge of, holding itself to the creed. Its core value is to help members achieve the creed. It chooses to believe in its values while sometimes questioning them. What it's for:
1. help participants reach their values
2. be a place of discourse and of spreading knowledge
3. give ritual, belief, and purpose
4. most importantly, help people both psychologically and in real ways

It defines the [practices](#practices). See [the Commons](the-commons.md).

### Practices
*— Sebi*

Given by [the Commons](#the-commons), according to its values. Its goal is to deeply understand how to help people achieve the creed. A member can reject any practice, and the Commons treats that as feedback.

### Membership
*— Sebi*

Belief, practice, or both? Could someone who doubts but practices belong? I don't know yet. Probably.

### Belief vs. dedication
*— Sebi*

There is believing the statement, and there is dedicating yourself to doing the things it describes. Are both the creed? Unsettled.

### Words tied to religion
*— Sebi*

My girlfriend is Christian and is going to help with this. Words like "religion" and "church" may feel wrong or even insulting. It still serves the purpose of a religion, but the English words for these tools are closely tied to religion. These views need not counteract any religion. This is meant to sit alongside them. Hence the [names](#names).

### Names
*— Sebi, with Claude*

- **Understanding**, not "truth". Truth isn't binary; it's a structure we can never reach. Calling one section "truth" sounds like we're blind to everything else. *(Claude)* Truth is what we aim at; understanding is what we hold.
- **Will**, not "agency" or "authenticity". Integrity was rejected: it's consistency with values already set, external to inward, while this is inward to outward, choosing your steps with care.
- **The Commons**, not "the church". See [words tied to religion](#words-tied-to-religion).
- **Reflections**, not "loomings" (from Moby-Dick). The equivalent of sermons: things to bring back faith, interpret the creed, and so on.
- The whole thing still has no name.

### Reflections start with limitations
*— Sebi*

In the spirit of the creed, a reflection opens with the speaker's limitations going in, so listeners know what the speaker has assumed. Be upfront. There is one on [convergence](#convergence) and one on [suffering](#suffering).

### Recording reasoning
*— Sebi*

Keep track of reasoning as we go, so it can be re-examined later. Not every page needs reasoning, but every decision needs it somewhere. Some pages are like Stoa's data pages: things made after reasoning that lives elsewhere. Page shape: point, short assumptions, the piece, unresolved (before the justification, so readers can ponder it), justification. The creed stands bare, as the starting point, but needs its own justification page somewhere. Written in the [documentation standard](documentation-standard.md).

### Kinds of assumption
*— Sebi*

Values and goals are a kind of assumption. So are beliefs about the world used to reach conclusions. Because reasoning is mostly inductive, the reasoning itself is assumed valid. And many things are deduced as the right thing to do given our role. There's no clear line between these. Which belong up front, and which will show up in the justification? A method for choosing would be very nice, for Stoa too.
- Values aren't false. Ask "would the piece change or exist without this goal?"
- "Is it argued for on this page?" doesn't help the writer, who hasn't written the argument yet.
- Conclusions from other pages go in assumptions as "relies on".
- You can argue for an assumption all the way down. At what level is it core?

It feels more incomplete the more I think about it. We can't see every assumption, put every reason into words, or notice every open question, but recording what we can is still valuable, so we continue.

### Morning practice
*— Sebi, with Claude*

See [morning](../Will/practices/morning.md). Notes from making it:
- Sitting is for understanding; standing up is the act of taking charge.
- The words shouldn't be locked in. They should come on the spot, from what I want, what I value, and where I've been losing, with the main themes kept. But unprompted I got stuck, even with the ideas in my head, so we use fixed openings with a default *(Claude)*.
- An ending like "amen": "Today, I strive again." The pattern is "(time period), (I or we) strive again."
- Style: punchy and active, not passive. Personal and meaningful, serious but not formal. "I recognize" and "yet" count as serious, not formal.

### Wanting to want
*— Sebi, with Claude*

We often have second-order wants: we want to want something because it aligns with our goals and values, but we don't want to do it. What practice could help make myself want to do something?
- *(Claude)* The trap: ignoring the thing brings relief, while working on it but "not enough" brings the bad feeling back. Avoidance gets rewarded.
- Deciding it's a lost cause can be easier than trying, and that can be an escape from blame rather than an honest judgment.
- *(Claude)* A draft practice exists in conversation: say the dread plainly, take one small step, and decide beforehand that it's enough. To be continued.

### Two unreachable ideals
*— Sebi*

Maybe **truth** is the whole structure of the universe, including all possible relations inside it, and **agency** is absolute free will. We can never reach either. So [Understanding](#understanding-or-searching-for-truth) is the practice of seeking truth, and [Will](#will-or-taking-charge) is the act of using our limited agency. Something like that. It fits [names](#names), and might be the connection in [the pillars feel like one thing](#the-pillars-feel-like-one-thing).

- *(Claude)* An asymmetry: truth seems real but out of reach, while absolute free will may not make sense at all. A choice free of every influence would have no reasons behind it. Maybe the ideal is instead acting only from influences you have examined and endorsed.

### Ideal, practice, and what we have
*— Sebi, with Claude*

Builds on [two unreachable ideals](#two-unreachable-ideals). Maybe a line of three:

| ideal (never reached) | practice | what we have and grow |
|---|---|---|
| truth | ? | understanding |
| sovereignty | ? | agency |

- "Freedom" was closer than absolute free will, but it implies doing whatever you want, rather than doing what you believe you ought to and being the person you want to be. "Sovereignty" might be ok, but sounds a bit cold.
- "Knowledge" implies something fixed and certain, so understanding fits better as what we *have*.
- Agency over will: will implies pure willpower, while a big part of agency is listening to impulses you have trained. This may mean renaming the [Will](#will-or-taking-charge) section.
- Not settled on any of this yet.
- Maybe "striving" is the practice for both: striving for truth, striving for sovereignty (or whatever the ideal is). See [striving](#striving).
- The practice words are still missing. Rejected: "seeking / shaping". *(Claude)* Also floated: inquiry, cultivation, discipline, striving.
- *(Claude)* Agency is the capacity to act; will is that capacity pointed somewhere, with effort.

### Striving
*— Sebi*

The creed is about striving toward ideals we can never reach. So "striving" might be a name for the whole thing, something like "strivinity". It might also be the word for prayers. It already ends the [morning practice](#morning-practice): "Today, I strive again." Relates to [ideal, practice, and what we have](#ideal-practice-and-what-we-have) and the open question in [names](#names).
