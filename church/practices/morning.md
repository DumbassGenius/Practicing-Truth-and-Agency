# Point
To rededicate oneself to the creed each morning, in a few minutes, and turn that into action the same day. For now it is made for one person, the founder.

# Assumptions
**Values**
- Dedication to the creed.
- Honesty: say only what can be meant, even in doubt.
- The language is personal and meaningful: active, with punch, and serious without being formal.

**Relies on**
- [The Creed](../../creed/creed.md) (not yet written)
- [The Pillars](../../creed/pillars.md) (not yet written)
- [Faith](../../creed/faith.md) (not yet written)

**Premises**
- Repeated acts shape a person, and habit and intuition are the most powerful resource we have for living by our values.
- We can never be completely free of bias and influence.
- What the body does can carry meaning.
- A short practice is easier to keep than a long one.

# The piece

**Sit. Take three slow breaths.** Then say:

> I recognize my beliefs are and will always be an approximation of the vast structure of reality, shaped by influences I can never fully see or escape. And so I allow my understanding to be challenged, and I rededicate myself to refining my approximation, to understanding those influences, and to using my understanding to plan my steps with care.



**Stand.** Then say:

> Knowing this, I yet choose to act. I will shape the influences that shape me, so that my values come naturally. When the moment comes, I will act on my beliefs and my gut with conviction, and look back only to understand and improve.

> I have faith that striving for truth and agency will lead me and my fellow humans to live lives worth living, and to add value to each other and the world beyond us. Today I choose to strive again.

**Write** one value, and an if-then plan for living it: "if X happens, I will Y."

**Enter the "if"** right away, if possible.

# Unresolved
- Who is this said to? Oneself, with humanity as witness, was suggested but not settled.
- If a habit works by not being examined, how do we notice when it has gone wrong?
- When should the practice itself be questioned? A set time, such as weekly, was suggested.
- Should there be a hand gesture? Opening the hands while seated and closing them while standing was suggested. It is left out for now because it was never settled.
- Is "agency" a better name for the pillar called "authenticity"?
- Do honest seekers converge on values? Different journeys, with no force guiding them to the same place, may still share basic values. This belongs to the creed, but it came up here.

# Justification
**Sacred and questionable.** Doing something to move toward the creed fulfils the creed, so the act is sacred. Admitting it may be the wrong practice also follows the creed, so questioning it is sacred too. We do it despite our questioning, because that is the only way to push through.

**Sitting, then standing.** Sitting is for knowing, doubt, and care. Standing is the act of taking charge. Care belongs to planning, and conviction to acting.

**Acting on an approximation.** Uncertainty is a reason to keep learning, not a reason to stop acting. In the moment of acting we do not question our beliefs, though we may reconsider them afterward. This has something of absurdism in it. Descartes' lost traveler, who picks a direction and walks straight, is a close relative.

**"I recognize."** Without it the line is a statement. With it, it is an action. Likewise, every part has active verbs.

**"Shape the influences that shape me."** No one is fully mindful all the time. The best we can do is shape our habits and intuition at every step we can. "Shaped by influences" in the seated part is answered here.

**"Gut."** Chosen for its punch. "Nature" could mean impulses we have not shaped, and "intuition" had less force.

**"Look back only to understand and improve."** We look back to learn, not to dwell. "Neutral" was considered, but sounded detached.

**"Truth and agency."** "Action" was close, but a ball rolling downhill moves too. "Integrity" is consistency with values already set, where agency is attending to what affects you and choosing.

**"Me and my fellow humans."** This gives warmth and recognizes that everyone is like us. "Brothers and sisters" could sound like members only. "Walks it with me" implied everyone shares one journey, so the walking metaphor was dropped.

**"Add value to each other and the world beyond us."** The creed promises this for everyone who lives this way, so the practice says it too.

**The if-then plan.** It ties a value to a situation and a response, so that acting on it can become habit. The evidence is modest and was not gathered on practices like this one: [values affirmation shows small effects](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25133846/), and [one trial](https://core.ac.uk/download/42612494.pdf) found that if-then plans helped where a values essay did not.
